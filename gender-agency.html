Decisions and stories

Who changes the plot?

15 selected turning points are initiated by women, 18 by men, and 1 by a fairy.

The study codes one illustrative turning point per section. Badoura continues the journey in disguise and later accounts for her actions before King Armanos. Parizade works out how to withstand the voices on the mountain and uses what she learns to rescue her brothers. In both cases, practical reasoning changes the outcome.

Who gets to tell?

21 of 23 headed in-character tellings are assigned to men, including seven voyages told by Sindbad. Scheherazade is the immediate teller of the other 2.

The heading count tracks sections presented as a character’s sustained narration. The frame gives Scheherazade the initiative and the audience for the sequence. The register also gives examples of storytelling within sections, including Badoura’s explanations to several listeners. It distinguishes speech presented directly from accounts summarized by the narrator.

Men have greater textual visibility and dominate the headed first-person and in-character tellings. The decisions show a different dimension of power. Women negotiate terms, withhold information, test risks and exercise judgment, sometimes while their freedom is constrained. The decision tally depends on the turning point selected in each section. Several coded sections belong to the same cycle, so the seven Sindbad voyages give that character more opportunities to appear.
