Before Night 70

The discovery

A fisherman pulls a locked chest from the Tigris. At the palace, the caliph’s party opens it and finds a murdered woman. Shahrazad’s telling stops at dawn.

What the account establishes
A woman has been killed. The people who find her have yet to identify her or explain her death.
What the pause changes
The discovery reaches Shahryar before any explanation. Dinarzad’s response and Shahrazad’s promise of more make listening itself part of the scene.
Source note

Haddawy, The Story of the Three Apples, opening and dawn break before Night 70. Scene paraphrased.

Night 70

Two confessions

The caliph gives his vizier Ja’far three days to find the killer. As Ja’far and his relatives face execution, a young man confesses. An older man makes the same claim. Ja’far objects when the caliph orders both men hanged.

What changes
The young man knows how the body was wrapped. His detailed account gives the caliph a reason to ask how the killing happened.
What remains open
The details support his claim to know what happened to the body. His explanation of the killing has yet to be heard.
Source note

Haddawy, The Story of the Three Apples, Night 70, the gallows and the two confessions. Scene paraphrased.

Night 71 · The husband speaks

The husband’s account

The young man says the woman was his wife. During an illness she asked him for an apple, and he brought three from Basra. Later, a man carrying an apple claims he received it from her. The husband finds only two at home. When his wife says she does not know where the third is, he takes her reply as confirmation and kills her.

What the object shows
One apple is missing. Its absence leaves open how it changed hands.
What can be heard of her perspective?
Her request expresses a desire and prompts the journey. Her answer states the limit of her knowledge. Both reach the caliph through the husband, who gives her answer a meaning she never states.
Source note

Haddawy, The Story of the Three Apples, Night 71, the wife’s request, the encounter in the market and the husband’s return. Scene paraphrased.

Night 71 · A voice within the account

The child’s account

The husband recounts what his eldest son told him after the killing. The boy took an apple from home. A man snatched it and heard the boy explain where it had come from. The husband says he then understood that he had killed his wife unjustly.

What changes
The boy’s account supplies a route for the apple and explains how the man could know about the journey to Basra.
What remains open
The caliph hears the boy’s words through the husband. The man who took the apple has yet to speak in the inquiry.
Source note

Haddawy, The Story of the Three Apples, Night 71, the son’s account and dawn break before Night 72. Scene paraphrased.

Night 72

The apple returns

Facing another deadline, Ja’far notices an apple in his daughter’s possession. She tells him she bought it from Rayhan, a man enslaved in his household. Rayhan admits taking the apple from the boy. Before the caliph, Ja’far makes the telling of another tale conditional on a pardon.

What changes
The daughter’s knowledge of the purchase leads Ja’far to Rayhan. Her account and Rayhan’s admission supply the missing links in the apple’s path.
What remains unheard
The apple’s route can now be reconstructed. The murdered woman’s experience is still available only through the words and accounts the tale gives others.

How does the daughter’s small piece of knowledge change the inquiry? What would the wife’s own account have allowed the listeners to reconsider?

Source note

Haddawy, The Story of the Three Apples, Night 72, Ja’far’s daughter, Rayhan’s account and the transition to The Story of the Two Viziers. Scene paraphrased.
