The Fisherman and the Demon

What can a question change when one speaker holds the power to kill the other? Follow the fisherman from his first encounter with the demon to their bargain.

Nights 9–10

The sealed jar

A fisherman draws a brass jar from the sea and opens its seal. Smoke pours out and takes the shape of a demon. The demon tells him that, after centuries in confinement, he vowed to kill whoever released him. The fisherman appeals to him to spare the man who freed him. The demon insists on carrying out the threat.

What does the fisherman know?
He has seen smoke emerge from the jar and take shape. He also has the demon’s account of his imprisonment and has heard him refuse an appeal for mercy.
What changes when he asks to speak?
He asks the demon to answer one question truthfully. The demon agrees to hear it. Shahrazad’s telling pauses at dawn before the fisherman puts the question.
Source note

Haddawy, The Story of the Fisherman and the Demon, the opening encounter before Night 10 and the exchange at the close of Night 10. Scene paraphrased.

Night 11

The demonstration

The fisherman questions how the demon’s whole body could have fit inside the jar. The demon turns into smoke, enters the vessel and calls out from inside to prove his claim. The fisherman immediately replaces the sealed stopper.

What does the question accomplish?
It draws the demon into demonstrating a claim about himself. The fisherman’s act of closing the jar shows how he turns the demonstration into an opportunity to survive.
Who controls what happens next?
The demon is confined again. The fisherman can now listen to his pleas and decide whether to return the jar to the sea or open it.
Source note

Haddawy, The Story of the Fisherman and the Demon, Night 11, the question about the jar and the resealing. Scene paraphrased.

Night 18 · After the tale of King Yunan and the Sage Duban

The bargain

The fisherman returns to the demon’s plea after telling the story of King Yunan and the Sage Duban. He threatens to throw the jar back into the sea. The demon promises to spare and reward him. The fisherman requires a solemn pledge before opening the jar. When the demon kicks the jar away, the fisherman fears betrayal and calls on him to remember his promise.

What does the pledge change?
It gives the fisherman terms for releasing the demon. Once the jar is out of reach, he can appeal to the demon’s word and to the consequences of breaking it.
What role does the embedded tale play?
The fisherman recalls the sage’s appeal to the king. By telling and then returning to that story, he gives the demon another example through which to consider how one person answers another’s plea.

Time to speak again

The fisherman’s question makes the demon stop and demonstrate his claim. At Shahrazad’s first dawn, her unfinished tale gives Shahryar a reason to postpone his decision. Both exchanges show how speaking and listening can change what happens next.
