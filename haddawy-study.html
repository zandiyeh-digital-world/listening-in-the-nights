Sources and counting rules

Husain Haddawy, translator, The Arabian Nights, New Deluxe Edition, W. W. Norton, 2008, based on Muhsin Mahdi’s edition. Page links refer to PDF viewer pages. The full Baghdad cycle covers pp. 110–208. The household and court scope excludes the dervishes’ accounts, from the first dervish’s heading on p. 134 to the household scene that resumes on p. 187. The Three Apples covers pp. 209–215 and ends before the next embedded story. Baghdad source · Three Apples source

Word totals use extracted narrative text, including verse, night transitions and embedded dialogue. The scan removes bracketed headings at page starts and truncates numbered footnotes. The standalone footnote continuation on p. 133 is omitted. Adjective counts use a fixed list and two patterns, immediately before a gendered noun or after selected linking verbs with a gendered noun or pronoun. These are candidate frequencies. Some matches are generic, repeated, or conditional, and the scan can miss coordinated descriptions, descriptions across a page break and references by name. Page links let readers inspect the wording.

Quoted segments count text enclosed in paired double quotation marks. They include framing dialogue and quoted material inside testimony. Extended accounts, which can continue in unquoted first-person narration, are recorded separately. Quoted words measure the amount of quotation across all speakers. Gender-specific speaker totals have only been verified for the two focal women in The Three Apples.

The cast roster counts identifiable individual humans listed in the register. It counts transformed sisters once each and excludes supernatural figures and collective roles such as the forty women and the one-eyed men. The action tally is a curated comparison of plot turns, including disclosures that change what happens, with a shared action counted once. These descriptive counts apply to Haddawy’s translation. They are separate from the Lang corpus.
