The frame narrative · Prologue through Night 2

Shahrazad and Shahryar

Shahrazad plans a first telling with her sister Dinarzad. Follow their choices from the request for a story to the king’s decision to hear it continue.

The frame narrative concerns a king’s violence against his wives.

Prologue

The request

Before she goes to the king, Shahrazad asks Dinarzad to request a story at a particular moment. Once summoned to the room, Dinarzad waits until everyone is awake and asks her sister for a tale before they part at daybreak.

What Shahrazad anticipates
A request made at the right moment can create a reason for her to speak. She tells Dinarzad that the plan is meant to save her life and end the king’s practice.
What Dinarzad contributes
Dinarzad judges when to speak in the room. She presents the story as a way for the sisters to spend their remaining time together, giving Shahrazad an opening to address the king.
Source note

Haddawy, The Arabian Nights, Prologue, Shahrazad’s instructions to Dinarzad and Dinarzad’s request before Night 1. Scene paraphrased.

Night 1

The first telling

With Shahryar’s permission, Shahrazad begins the story of a merchant. A demon blames him for his son’s death and demands his life. The merchant pleads that the death was accidental. The demon raises his sword.

What the story puts before Shahryar
Shahryar hears an accusation followed by an explanation and a plea. The choice of tale places a demand for immediate punishment within the story he has agreed to hear.
What a listener can reconsider
The merchant’s plea introduces intent as a question. The scene holds that question open as the demon prepares to act.
Source note

Haddawy, The Story of the Merchant and the Demon, Night 1, the accusation and the merchant’s plea. Scene paraphrased.

Dawn before Night 2

The first dawn

Morning interrupts Shahrazad just as the demon is about to strike. Dinarzad responds with admiration. Shahrazad promises a more entertaining continuation if the king lets her live. Shahryar decides to spare her long enough to hear the rest.

How Shahrazad uses the interruption
The narrative marks dawn as the moment she falls silent. Her response turns the unfinished story into a reason for Shahryar to wait.
What the king’s decision establishes
Curiosity changes the timing of his decision. His stated intention is to hear the rest and then have her killed, so the danger continues into the next night.
Source note

Haddawy, The Story of the Merchant and the Demon, close of Night 1 and the exchange before Night 2. Scene paraphrased.

Night 2

The next night

Dinarzad asks for a tale again. This time Shahryar asks for the merchant’s story in particular. Shahrazad takes up the moment when the demon raises his sword. The merchant asks for time to settle his affairs and say goodbye.

What changes in the room
Dinarzad opens the exchange as before. Shahryar now asks for a particular continuation, showing that the previous night’s telling has shaped what he wants to hear.
How the two stories meet
Shahrazad has gained a night to continue. Inside her story, the merchant also asks for time. The parallel invites the reader to consider what can happen once an immediate decision is deferred.
Source note

Haddawy, The Story of the Merchant and the Demon, the request for the continuation and the opening of Night 2. Scene paraphrased.

Sources and interpretation

The scenes follow Husain Haddawy’s translation of The Arabian Nights, New Deluxe Edition, based on the edition of Muhsin Mahdi. The reading covers the end of the Prologue and the beginning of The Story of the Merchant and the Demon. Consulted edition

The commentary draws on Zahra Zandiyeh’s Teaching through Tales, MA research paper, University of Guelph, Fall 2025. The four stages are an editorial division for this reading.
