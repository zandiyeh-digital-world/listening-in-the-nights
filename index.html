Research and methods

The exhibition draws on Zahra Zandiyeh’s Teaching through Tales: Storytelling and Female Instructive Power in Husain Haddawy’s The Arabian Nights, a Major Research Paper in English at the University of Guelph (Fall 2025), supervised by Dr. Jennifer Schacker. Zandiyeh reads these four episodes through feminist narratology, postcolonial feminist thought, and Arabic literary criticism. Her analysis of suspense, timing, and silence guides the exhibition’s close readings.

Close reading
The four chapters follow Husain Haddawy’s translation of The Arabian Nights, New Deluxe Edition, based on Muhsin Mahdi’s Arabic edition. The chapters paraphrase selected scenes and identify the relevant nights. Consulted Haddawy edition
Two tales in Haddawy
We extracted text from the PDF and defined the story boundaries by page. A fixed list and grammatical patterns identify candidate adjectives linked to female and male references. We compare Baghdad’s household and court episodes with the full cycle to show the effect of including the dervishes’ tales. The study also counts quoted text, lists individually identified characters, and documents selected plot decisions. Explore the two-tale comparison
Whole-volume analysis in Lang
We analyzed 34 narrative sections of Andrew Lang’s English selection from Project Gutenberg, excluding the contents, preface, headings, and end matter. The analysis counts adjectives before gendered nouns and after selected linking verbs. Context review connects some matches to characters and their aliases. Each word opens the recorded evidence. Each statistical study reports results for its own edition. Quoted word counts, descriptions, and selected actions measure different aspects of representation. The study pages give the counting rules, source links, and downloadable data.
