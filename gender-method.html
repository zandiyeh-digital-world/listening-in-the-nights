Sources and method

Andrew Lang, selector and editor, The Arabian Nights Entertainments, Longmans, Green and Co., 1918 (1898), as identified by Project Gutenberg eBook #128. The analysis inspected all 34 headed narrative sections in the unpaginated plain text, excluding headings, preface, contents and end matter. The digital section titles locate each entry. Browse the source’s linked table of contents. Edition record · Analyzed text

Lang’s abridged English selection is the corpus for these counts. The exhibition’s close readings use Husain Haddawy’s translation. Lang’s Baghdad cycle ends with Zobeida dismissing the guests after the third Calender’s account. The two women’s extended court accounts discussed in the exhibition’s Baghdad chapter belong to the Haddawy reading.

The working roster and decision annotations involve editorial judgment. An independent second coding pass would test borderline character identities and whether the decision pattern holds when more than one action is counted per section.

How descriptions were counted

All 26 existing linking-verb matches with a noun or pronoun subject were checked in context. Twenty-two were linked to a cast entry. Four remain without a cast link because the reference is collective or has no corresponding individual in the roster. Before-a-noun matches retain their original labels. Character filtering uses verified cast links.

The same fixed adjective list is used in both searches. The first looks immediately before a selected gendered noun. The second looks for an explicit gendered noun or she or he followed by a listed linking verb and one or more adjectives. It allows common modifiers and coordinated adjectives. The 29 predicate candidates were reviewed and 26 retained. Negated descriptions are counted as adjective mentions and flagged in the passage view. A third search uses 47 character entries and their aliases from the cast roster, with separate story scopes for the two Aminas. Four matches passed contextual review. All four refer to men, and two describe a conditional or mistaken belief about death. Zero female matches in this search reflects the wording and limits of the rule. The counts cover selected forms and leave many indirect descriptions outside the search. The counts combine narrator and character speech.
